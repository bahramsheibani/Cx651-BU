(2) plot-fail_rate.png: t.out (the first-fit allocator with no splitting/coalescing)
holds a flat, low fail rate of ~0.1 across every allocation size tested (2^5 to
2^8). t2.out (test-coalesce, which splits blocks on malloc and coalesces
adjacent free blocks on free) starts at the same ~0.1 fail rate for small

plot-throughput.png: both allocators track each other closely and both
increase in allocations/sec as the max allocation size grows. t.out and
t2.out are nearly identical at 2^5 and 2^6 (crossing over between them), but
t2.out pulls modestly ahead of t.out at the larger sizes (2^7 and 2^8).

test-coalesce (t2.out) has the better throughput overall, since it is
ahead at 3 of the 4 tested sizes (2^5, 2^7, 2^8) and only trails slightly at
2^6. Splitting blocks lets a single mmap'd arena satisfy more, smaller
requests without needing additional mmap calls, and coalescing keeps the
free list from filling up with unusably tiny fragments, so on average fewer
list nodes need to be traversed to find a fit, which keeps allocations/sec
high even as request sizes grow.

(3) test (t.out) has the better (lower) fail rate, staying flat at ~0.1
regardless of allocation size, while test-coalesce's fail rate rises sharply
with size. Because test never splits a block, every free block stays as
large as it was originally, so a block that was once big enough to satisfy
a request remains big enough later. test-coalesce, on the other hand, splits
blocks into a used piece and a leftover free piece on every allocation; as
the max allocation size increases, more of the 2048-byte arena gets carved
into small leftover slivers (each with its own header overhead) that are
too small to satisfy the next large request, so despite coalescing on free,
the arena fragments faster than it can be repaired, driving the fail rate up.
